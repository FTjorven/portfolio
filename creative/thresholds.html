The idea

What if a shader wasn't a flat picture, but a place you could walk into?

Shadertoy is full of whole worlds that only ever live on a flat canvas. In Thresholds each one sits behind an archway. The shader doesn't paint the doorway like a poster: every pixel gets the real ray from your eye, so the world behind it has depth and shifts as you move. Walk through and you're inside it, weightless, flying where you look.

Around that I built a small game. Three lights were lost to the worlds beyond the arches: tune each world, find its light, carry it home and place it on the dais. With all three home, a fourth archway rises and leads you out.

How it plays

Three worlds, one way home.

  1. 01 · Tune

    Turn the dial

    Each world hides its light until it's tuned right. The dial beside the archway changes a value in its shader: the fractal formula of Star Nest, the time of day over the sea, the size of Creation's light. The carving on the arch shows where it has to point.

  2. 02 · Enter

    Step through

    Walking through the opening puts you inside the world: a big sphere follows you, painted from the inside with the same shader, now with the rays starting at your eye.

  3. 03 · Return

    Look back

    The doorway home shows the ruin from your exact viewpoint: it's rendered into a texture and mapped back onto the doorway in screen space, so it lines up perfectly, even out at sea.

  4. 04 · Bring it home

    Wake the dais

    Each light has its own socket. Placing it plays a Blender animation: a stone ring rises, turns and hangs tilted above the dais, and the ruin brightens. The third one raises the final archway.

The worlds

Shadertoy classics, ported to WGSL.

The way home uses Heartfelt by Martijn Steinrucken (BigWings). The ports, the 3D ray setup and everything around them are mine.

What's inside

Built for the GPU, down to the dust.

WebGPU · TSL / WGSL

Shaders with depth

Three.js WebGPURenderer. Each shader gets a ray origin and direction from the 3D scene instead of a pixel position.

Compute shader

450 motes of dust

Drift into the doorways, simulated entirely on the GPU in a storage buffer. Something WebGL can't do.

Rapier physics

Things to throw

Crystals, urns and stones to pick up and throw. Throw one through a doorway to energize that world; hit something hard and it shatters.

Blender

Baked and animated

The archways and dais are modelled by a Python script, with stone colour and ambient occlusion baked into one texture, and four animations driven by the game.

Web Audio

No sound files

Music, humming doorways, footsteps and shattering, all generated in code, with positional audio from where it happens.

Under the hood

The trick: give every pixel a real ray.

script.js (simplified)
const createWorldMaterial = (world, rayOrigin) => {
  const shader = wgslFn(world.shader);
  const material = new MeshBasicNodeMaterial();
  material.colorNode = shader({
    rayOrigin,
    rayDir: positionWorld.sub(cameraPosition).normalize(),
    iTime: world.time,
    tuning: world.tuning, // the dial
  });
  return material;
};

// seen through the doorway: rays start on the doorway
world.doorwayMaterial = createWorldMaterial(world, positionWorld);
// inside the world: rays start at your eye
world.insideMaterial = createWorldMaterial(world, cameraPosition);

The same shader runs twice: on the doorway, and on the inside of a sphere that follows you once you've walked through. Only the ray's starting point changes, which is what makes stepping through feel continuous.

dust.js (simplified)
// runs on the GPU for all 450 motes, every frame
const update = Fn(() => {
  const position = positions.element(instanceIndex);
  const energy = energyOf(doorwayOf(instanceIndex));

  // drawn in slowly, much faster when the world is energized
  const pull = float(0.2).add(energy.mul(1.5));
  position.addAssign(vec3(0, 0, pull.negate()).add(steer).add(wander).mul(delta));

  // through the doorway: start over, out in front
  If(position.z.lessThan(0.02), () => {
    position.assign(spawn(instanceIndex, elapsed.mul(60).toUint(), SPAWN_NEAR));
  });
})().compute(count);
Hardest part

Making the step through a doorway seamless: the world behind it, the sphere around you and the view of the ruin behind you all have to agree on the same rays, every frame.

What I took away

Working with WebGPU and TSL from the ground up: porting shaders to WGSL, writing compute passes, and fitting Blender, physics and generated sound into one experience.

Step inside Needs WebGPU: a recent Chrome, Edge or Safari on desktop.
