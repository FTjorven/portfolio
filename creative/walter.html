Designing

The brief

MoMu wanted interactive stories that make people curious about fashion and want to visit, one designer per story.

I chose Walter Van Beirendonck, and one strange chapter in his career: in 1999 he left Mustang, and his contract stopped him from using his own name for five seasons. So he kept designing as aestheticterrorists, was accused of copying… himself, and eventually came back as aestheticterrorists by Walter.

My angle: don't just tell the reader about losing a name, make them feel it. The story opens by asking for your name, and then takes it away.

Interactions

You don't just read it, you take part.

01

Sign your name away

You type your name to "start designing". On submit, the line under it is erased with DrawSVG and the page answers: your name is gone, it was signed away. From there on, Walter's story is also yours.

02

Collections in the crosshairs

A pinned, scroll-scrubbed section moves through four collections from 1999 to 2002. The year sits in a target, and each title and description types itself out with TextPlugin as you scroll.

03

Detonate a visual bomb

Walter's work drops loud colours and slogans into quiet fashion. Here the reader literally does the same: hit Detonate and the page explodes into a manifesto.

04

Pull the slogan lever

A slot machine built from Walter's vocabulary. Pull the lever and three reels spin into a new slogan, the kind he would print across a garment.

05

Uncover the headline

The plagiarism chapter ends in a newspaper reporting the reveal, with key words redacted. Click a black bar to uncover it, until you reach the last line: Walter Van Beirendonck is aestheticterrorists.

Visual language

Loud on purpose, like the clothes.

I built the look from Walter's own world: bright colour dropped into grey, cut-out halftone collage like protest posters, and heavy, shouting type next to a quiet editorial serif. Bombs, grenades, masks and crosshairs come straight from the aestheticterrorists name.

Development

Every chapter has its own interaction, all running on one GSAP setup.

  • Vite + npm to bundle the JavaScript and CSS, deployed to GitHub Pages.
  • ScrollTrigger for the pinned collection section and the reveal of every chapter.
  • DrawSVG to erase the line under your name, TextPlugin to type out the collections.
  • AVIF images for the collage cut-outs, to keep a heavy visual style fast.
  • Responsive layouts that recompose the collage for mobile instead of just shrinking it.
name-form.js (simplified)
form.addEventListener("submit", (e) => {
  e.preventDefault();

  gsap.timeline({ onComplete: showNameIsGone })
    // the line flashes orange…
    .to(line, { stroke: "#FF6B35", strokeWidth: 6, duration: 0.3 })
    // …and is erased, taking your name with it
    .to(line, { drawSVG: "0% 0%", duration: 1.2, ease: "power2.in" })
    .to(input, { opacity: 0, y: -20 }, "<");
});
Hardest part

Balancing a long-read with a lot of interaction: every interactive moment had to move the story forward, not interrupt the reading.

What I took away

Designing and building a full experience on my own, from concept and visual language to the last scroll animation.
