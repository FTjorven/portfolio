The brief

Build a desktop experience that tells a personal story by combining machine learning and animation.

I chose the half hour before a concert: the soundcheck. Nobody in the crowd sees it, but everything depends on it. The visitor takes over from the crew: record the instruments, balance the mix, design the lights and run the show. Instead of clicking through a form, you play real sounds into your microphone and the app reacts.

Brief → build

Every requirement, used for the story.

Machine learning

Two self-trained sound models

I trained two audio classifiers in Teachable Machine and run them in the browser with TensorFlow.js: one tells a guitar from a bongo, the other counts applause at the end.

ScrollTrigger

Story & pacing

Pins the intro story and moves you from stage to stage. The next stage only opens once both instruments have been recorded.

Draggable

A real mixing desk

Each channel is a vertical fader you drag with the mouse, with its value and accessibility attributes updated live.

MotionPath

Moving stage lights

Three light fixtures travel along curved rails above the stage, rotating with the path so their beams sweep the room.

Error handling

No dead ends

Loading states while models download, clear feedback when the microphone or a model fails, and buttons that stay disabled until there's something to play.

How it plays

Five stages, one show.

  1. 01 · Record

    Play into your microphone

    The app listens continuously. When the model is at least 90% sure it hears a guitar or a bongo, it starts recording a four-second clip of it, with no record button needed.

  2. 02 · Mix

    Balance the band

    Four draggable faders: kick, snare, guitar and vocals. Each one ticks off once you've adjusted it, and a visualiser confirms the final mix.

  3. 03 · Light

    Design the lights

    Pick a mood, then set intensity, beam width and effects step by step. Start the light show and the moving heads sweep across the screen.

  4. 04 · Show

    Run the final soundcheck

    A canvas stage where you trigger your own recordings and layer confetti, lasers, spotlights and smoke, with an intensity slider from chill to wild.

  5. 05 · Applause

    Clap for yourself

    You get a score for your recordings, mix, lighting and show. Then the second model listens for applause: every clap it hears fills a star.

Under the hood

The key moment: letting the model decide when to record.

ml5-instrument.js (simplified)
recognizer.listen(async (result) => {
  const i = result.scores.indexOf(Math.max(...result.scores));
  const label = labels[i].toLowerCase();
  const confidence = result.scores[i];

  // Only record when the model is 90% sure
  if (isInstrument(label) && confidence >= 0.9 && !isRecording) {
    isRecording = true;
    const clip = await recordSnippet(4); // seconds
    recordings[label] = clip;
    unlockNextStageIfComplete();
  }
});

The high threshold keeps talking and background noise from starting a recording. The clips are kept in memory and reused later in the show and in the final mix.

Hardest part

Training sound models that work in a real room: microphones, background noise and different instruments all change what the model hears.

What I took away

How to train and run machine learning in the browser, and how to use animation to guide someone through an experience instead of just decorating it.
