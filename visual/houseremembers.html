The brief

One everyday object. Three new uses. One story.

We live in a throwaway society, and our homes are full of things we forgot we had. The brief: pick a household object nearly everyone owns, come up with three alternative uses that fit one concept, and promote them on a website that takes the visitor on a journey.

I picked the deck of playing cards: the one wedged behind old coasters and tax receipts, waiting for a poker night that never comes. Instead of throwing it out, the concept dresses it up. Not for gambling this time, but for glamour: everything you need to host a party, made from a single deck.

Storytelling

Once, every card had a purpose.

Visual language

A card table, dressed for a party.

Everything comes from the card table: felt green, card red, warm cream like an old deck, and poker chips as buttons and markers. All illustrations are my own: flat shapes with a thick black outline and a hard offset shadow, so they feel like cut-out cards on the table. Junicode, a classic serif, gives the titles a vintage playing-card feel. Reddit Sans keeps the body text easy to read, and Bagnard adds an accent.

Three new uses

Prepare, invite, play.

01 · Prepare

Confetti cards

The Art of the First Cut. A hole punch turns old cards into speckled confetti: a sliver of the Queen's robe, the curl of the Joker's hat. The stapler illustration is clickable and bursts real confetti onto the page.

02 · Invite

DIY invites

An etiquette guide. Every suit is a message: a heart means come dressed in red and bring a dessert. A carousel goes through each suit, with the invitation itself designed as a playing card.

03 · Play

The Drinker's Deck

Pull a card, drink your fate. A party game with house rules: draw a card and the bartender serves the drink that matches it. Poker chips number the rules, and you can draw a card right on the site.

Reviews & event

Even the reviews are playing cards.

Each testimonial is signed by the card the guest drew, with its rank in the corners. The site ends by inviting you to a preview party, with a sign-up where you pick a mood and a party role by suit, and a final toast: one card for each act.

Hardest part

Making three quite different ideas feel like one story, and keeping the playing-card style going through every section without it turning into a casino.

What I took away

How much a clear concept and tone of voice carry a site. Once the deck was the story, every button, review and form field could follow from it.
