Case study · Motion design

Revinyl.

A 47-second promo film for BLOCK I, a Kickstarter product that plays your digital music through the turntable you already own.

My role
Concept, art direction, animation, sound
Tools
After Effects, Audition, Photoshop, Figma
Course
Motion 2 · 2026
Format
1920×1080 · 25 fps · 47 s

Sound on. Music and effects mixed in Audition.

The brief

Find a Kickstarter without a good promo video, and make one.

A Kickstarter campaign stands or falls by how the idea is sold, and without a visual it's hard for backers to picture the product. The brief: pick a campaign with a missing or weak video and make it a promo of 30 to 60 seconds, fully scored, without copyrighted music.

I picked Revinyl's BLOCK I: a puck that sits on your turntable and plays digital music through it. A clever product that is hard to explain in words, so the film has to show it: your streaming library, finally on your record player.

Process

From animatic to final render.

01

The problem

The film opens on a turntable that only plays records, while all your music lives on your phone. The grey block-out in the animatic became a halftone collage of a record player in front of the city.

02

The pitch, in one line

Digital music. Played through your turntable. The tonearm turns into a line that guides the eye through the film, past the city and into the tagline.

03

Into the music

Zooming into the record leads into the music itself: a jazz player, then a tiny figure walking inside the label as the record spins.

04

The call to action

The film lands on the product and one clear ask: Fund BLOCK I on Kickstarter.

Visual language

Old records, new colours.

The look mixes the two worlds of the product. Black-and-white halftone photo collage stands for the analogue side: records, turntables, old jazz. Flat, soft colour shapes in pastel coral, blue and yellow stand for the digital side, flowing through every scene and carrying the transitions.

  • Paper#FEFBEE
  • Coral#F19771
  • Periwinkle#9EA8E4
  • Butter#F2E691
  • Halftone#1D1D1B
Stills
Hardest part

Explaining a product in under a minute without a voice-over, so every scene had to show one clear idea and hand off smoothly to the next.

What I took away

How much timing and sound carry a film. Going from animatic to rough edit to final render taught me to lock the story first and polish later.
